🎯 What Jelibox-client Does

Teaching a computer to recognize objects in pictures normally means hours of clicking tiny points and typing coordinates into scripts. Jelibox-client removes that work. It is a normal desktop program: you open it, you point it at a folder of images, and you start drawing.

You drag a box around a car, a person, a defect, a product, or anything else you care about. If a shape is not a neat rectangle, you place points instead and get a polygon that hugs the outline of the object. Each shape gets a label — a plain word like "helmet" or "scratch" — and Jalibox-client keeps track of every annotation for you.

When your images are labelled, the tool turns them into a standard dataset that training software understands. You can export to YOLO, COCO, or Pascal VOC, or go one step further and train a model directly with Ultralytics, then use that fresh model to finish the rest of your images automatically.

It is built for people who work with images every day — quality inspectors, researchers, students, small business owners — and who do not want to write a single line of code to get a usable model.

🚀 Your First Project in Five Steps

The first time you open the app you will see an empty workspace. Here is the shortest path from empty window to finished dataset.

  • Add your images. Use the open or add-image action in the app and select a folder full of pictures. The thumbnails appear in a list so you can move through them quickly.
  • Create your labels. Before drawing, add the class names you plan to use, such as "person", "helmet", "crack", or "bottle". Every shape you draw will be assigned one of these names.
  • Draw your shapes. Choose the box tool and drag a rectangle over an object, or choose the polygon tool and click around its outline, closing the shape when you are done. Press the delete key to remove a mistake.
  • Try auto-labeling. Open the auto-label panel, type in the objects you are looking for, and let YOLO-World or one of your own models pre-fill the image. Review what it found, fix anything that is wrong, and keep the good results.
  • Export your dataset. Open the export section, choose YOLO, COCO, or Pascal VOC, and point the app at a destination folder. The result is a tidy dataset ready for training or sharing.

📁 Choosing the Right Export Format

New users often wonder which format to pick. The good news is that you can export more than once, so nothing is lost by trying a format and switching later.

YOLO

Choose YOLO when you plan to train with Ultralytics or any other modern detector. It stores each image's annotations in a small text file next to the image, with object positions written as fractions of the image size. This is the simplest format to train with.

COCO

Choose COCO when you need polygon outlines as well as boxes, or when you are sharing your dataset with a research group or an online platform that expects COCO. All annotations are stored in one structured file.

Pascal VOC

Choose Pascal VOC when you are feeding older training pipelines or a tool that specifically asks for XML annotation files. It is well understood and widely supported.

A simple rule of thumb: training a detector yourself? Use YOLO. Sharing with researchers? Use COCO. Working with an older tool? Use Pascal VOC.

💡 Tips for Better Results

  • Use short, consistent label names. Pick one word per object type and stick to it. "Car" and "cars" should not both exist in the same project.
  • Draw tight boxes. Let the rectangle touch the object's edges without extra background. Loose boxes teach the model to look at empty space.
  • Label an image fully before moving on. Half-labelled images can confuse training because the model is told that unmarked objects are not there.
  • Start auto-labeling small. Try a handful of images first, check the quality, and only then run it across a large folder.
  • Keep a backup folder. Copy your project folder occasionally so a mistake or a moved file does not cost you a day of work.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to install Python or any other software first?

No. Jelibox-client is a desktop application. Download it from the releases page, open it, and start working. Everything happens inside the app window.

How do I get the newest version?

Return to the releases page at any time and download the newest release listed at the top. Your existing projects stay on your computer and are not affected by downloading a new copy.

What happens if auto-labeling finds the wrong objects?

Just fix them. Auto-labeling is a head start, not a final answer. Delete wrong shapes, adjust boxes that are too loose, and add anything the model missed. Your corrections are what make the dataset valuable.

Can I use my own trained model for auto-labeling?

Yes. You can train a model with Ultralytics on your annotated images and then load that model back into the app to label new images. It is the fastest way to work through a large batch.

Can I export the same project in more than one format?

Yes. Your annotations stay inside the project, so you can export to YOLO today, then export the same work to COCO or Pascal VOC later without redoing anything.

Where do I go if something is not working?

Open the releases page and read the notes attached to the newest release. If the answer is not there, use the issue tracker on the same GitHub page to describe what happened and what you expected.
